Ribbon Architect is available as a free iOS app and in the browser. Scan or measure a gift, browse pattern suggestions made for its exact proportions, or construct your own designs. Then step-by-step instructions show you how to lay the ribbon on your gift. Gifts are modeled as convex polyhedra, so many non-cuboid gift shapes, such as prisms, work too and have their own interesting ribbon patterns. A ribbon (without twists) that is pulled tight follows a locally shortest path, a geodesic. This is a path on the surface of the gift that runs straight on faces and folds correctly over edges and loops back on itself. As you'll see in the app, geodesics can be much more interesting than the axis-aligned loops that people usually tie when wrapping a gift. Click "Browse geodesics" on the first screen of the app, to get a glimpse of the endless variety of geodesics. (It currently stops after 5'000 or earlier depending on available memory. In theory, a box has infinitely many distinct geodesics.) For further variety, you can combine these building blocks by inserting twists: When twisted, two strands of ribbon that follow sections of geodesics that cross, turn over and follow the other geodesic. This can join two geodesics into a single ribbon. Click any crossing to insert a twist (after selecting a pattern or after drawing ribbons in "draw your own" mode). Or follow the tutorial (the ? icon in the upper right corner). This is the first version. The browser version hasn't been tested on many browsers and devices.
