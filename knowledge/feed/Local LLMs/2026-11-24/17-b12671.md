metal : batch concat rows and copy them in vector chunks (#32824) * metal : batch concat rows and copy them in vector chunks The concat kernel copied one element per thread, so wide rows with many rows ran at a fraction of the memory bandwidth. Batch several rows per threadgroup and copy the contiguous dim-0 elements in 4-element chunks when the row is contiguous. Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-GGUF (Mac Studio, M5 Ultra, 256GB) * test : cover concat vector and misaligned row paths Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF (RTX 5090 32GB, DDR5 128GB) * metal : hoist the concat src1 row offset into scalars Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF (RTX 5090 32GB, DDR5 128GB) * metal : peel to align the concat vector copy Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF (RTX 5090 32GB, DDR5 128GB) * metal : vectorize concat rows along any dimension Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF (RTX 5090 32GB, DDR5 128GB) * metal : specialize concat with function constants Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF (RTX 5090 32GB, DDR5 128GB) * metal : drop the concat row-batch dispatch Batching several rows per threadgroup reduces the number of threads by the batch factor. On machines with a large GPU this leaves too few requests in flight and the copy becomes latency bound: M5 Ultra (36 CPU) f16 [1024,8,256,1] d=0 8.1 us -> 17.2 us f32 [4096,8,256,1] d=0 35.4 us -> 118.5 us f16 [4096,8,256,1] d=2 50.9 us -> 102.0 us An interleaved sweep of the (nth, nrptg) dispatch over the concat perf cases on an M2 Ultra and an M5 Ultra shows that the base dispatch is best or tied for every shape once the rows are vectorized, and that the gains of the previous commit come from the vector copy alone: M2 Ultra f16 [1024,8,256,1] d=0 32.4 us -> 17.0 us M5 Ultra f16 [1024,8,256,1] d=0 13.3 us -> 8.1 us Keep the vector copy and the function-constant specialization, and return to the original dispatch. Assisted-by: dsh:llama.cpp/ggml-org/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF (RTX 5090 32GB, DDR5 128GB) * metal : revert the concat function constants Specializing the concat kernel on the concat dimension and on whether a row is split across threadgroups with function constants makes the strided scalar path slower. Interleaved over the concat perf cases on an M5 Ultra, the specialized kernel is not faster than the plain one, and it costs up to 18% on shapes that are already at the memory bandwidth limit: f32 [2050,8,256,1] b=255 d=0 21.0 us -> 25.1 us f32 [2050,8,256,1] b=256 d=2 31.1 us -> 36.7 us f16 [4096,8,256,1] b=256 d=2 31.2 us -> 36.7 us Revert the specialization and keep copying rows with the runtime arguments. The concat change is now limited to the kernel itself.
